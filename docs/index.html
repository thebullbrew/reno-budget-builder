Reno Math

The make-ready rehab budget builder. Price the whole renovation room by room, keep cosmetics and systems in separate columns, and know your number before the contractor's quote hypnotizes you.

The method

The perfect deal is a grandmother house: ugly wallpaper and a 1970s kitchen on top of a good roof and a young furnace. Cosmetics are the discount. Systems are the risk.

So this tool keeps them in separate columns. If the systems column starts eating the budget, the deal isn't a cosmetic flip with a little paint — it's a construction project wearing a costume, and it should be priced like one.

Rule of thumb baked in everywhere below: a rehab budget without contingency is a wish, not a budget.

The verdict

How the math works (no black boxes)
  • Each line's cost = preset for its finish level × quantity (where the line has one). A typed custom number always wins over the preset.
  • Hard costs = every line added up, split into Cosmetics and Systems columns.
  • Padding = contingency % + GC/PM markup %, both applied to hard costs. All-in budget = hard costs + padding.
  • Max offer (70% rule) = ARV × 0.70 − all-in rehab − soft costs (closing, holding, resale). Offer above this and the flip margin is gone before the first dumpster arrives.
  • Projected profit = ARV − purchase price − all-in rehab − soft costs. A 15–20% margin of the ARV is the comfort zone; under 10% and one surprise owns you.
  • The systems-share flag trips when systems pass 40% of hard costs. The grandmother-house ideal is cosmetics-heavy: the ugly you can see is the discount you can price.
Presets are starting points, not quotes. They reflect typical U.S. investor-grade pricing — your market, your contractor, and the house's actual condition move every number. Walk the property with a contractor before money moves.
